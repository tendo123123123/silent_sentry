% --- ABSTRACT ---
\begin{abstract}
Persistent border surveillance in unstructured, semi-arid environments presents critical challenges for autonomous systems. Traditional static sensor networks suffer from terrain occlusion, while multi-agent robotics rely heavily on continuous Radio Frequency (RF) state-sharing, creating severe vulnerabilities in Electronic Warfare (EW) contested environments. This paper proposes a decentralized, partition-based architecture for Ackermann-steered Unmanned Ground Vehicles (UGVs) operating under strict Emission Control (EMCON). We introduce three key novelties: (1) a Spatially-Bounded Lévy Patrol (SBLP) algorithm that guarantees mathematically unpredictable, geo-fenced sector coverage with zero inter-agent communication; (2) a perception-agnostic, event-driven Zenoh middleware topology that bypasses standard DDS discovery storms; and (3) an offline-trained Reinforcement Learning (RL) policy for Elastic Geo-Fencing. To maintain macroscopic fleet cohesion without edge-to-edge mesh networking, a centralized Base Station RL agent continuously evaluates topological costmaps. Upon detecting a coverage breach, it calculates mathematically optimal reallocation coordinates and broadcasts a single encrypted micro-burst, dynamically stretching the remaining fleet's SBLP parameters to autonomously "heal" the patrol net. Validated via OS-level network emulation and high-fidelity simulated environments, the system demonstrates robust, radio-silent navigation using LIO-SAM under simulated GPS denial.
\end{abstract}

% --- SECTION I: INTRODUCTION ---
\section{Introduction}
Current border security relies heavily on physical fencing and static camera towers, which suffer from line-of-sight occlusion in rolling dune terrains. While Unmanned Aerial Systems (UAS) provide rapid response, they lack endurance and are highly susceptible to catastrophic drift during GNSS denial events. 

While Unmanned Ground Vehicles (UGVs) offer the required persistence, current State-of-the-Art (SOTA) multi-agent frameworks fail in contested domains due to a critical dependency: the continuous RF emission of standard ROS 2 DDS networks, which enables adversary triangulation. We propose the "Silent Sentry" architecture, transitioning from cooperative swarms to an Independent Sentinel Network. By synthesizing biological foraging mathematics, connectionless middleware, and deep reinforcement learning, we present a system capable of unpredictable, fully autonomous operations with a zero-idle RF footprint.

% --- SECTION II: RELATED WORKS & NOVELTY ---
\section{Related Works \& Statement of Novelty}

\subsection{Multi-Agent Coverage and Zero-Communication Constraints}
Standard multi-robot area coverage algorithms require continuous state-sharing via mesh networks, making them vulnerable to EW jamming. While biological Lévy flights ($P(l) \sim l^{-\beta}$) have been adapted for swarm robotics, they are predominantly applied to holonomic agents in unconstrained spaces. We introduce the SBLP algorithm, which guarantees unpredictable sector coverage by integrating Ackermann kinematic smoothing and geo-fenced rejection sampling, eliminating the need for digital coordination.

\subsection{Stealth Network Topologies in ROS 2}
The DDS standard assumes reliable, high-bandwidth networks, utilizing continuous UDP multicast. We weaponize the Eclipse Zenoh protocol as a deliberate EMCON tool. By physically tying RF transmission states to a standardized perception trigger, we present a resilient, hardware-coupled network topology designed explicitly for radio-silent operations.

\subsection{Macroscopic Cohesion via RL}
To resolve the geographic vulnerability created when an agent abandons its sector, traditional swarms use mesh-network consensus algorithms. We introduce an offline-trained RL policy at the centralized Base Station to evaluate topographic costmaps and broadcast mathematically optimized reallocation coordinates, enabling self-healing coverage without edge-to-edge communication.

% --- SECTION III: SYSTEM ARCHITECTURE ---
\section{System Architecture}

\subsection{Digital Twin \& Hardware Specifications}
To ensure Sim-to-Real viability, the digital twin mirrors a physical Ackermann-steered chassis optimized for edge-compute constraints:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Compute:} NVIDIA Jetson Orin Nano Super.
    \item \textbf{Perception Stack:} Robosense RS-Helios-32 (32-channel LiDAR) coupled with a BMI088 6-axis IMU and an Electro-Optical/Infrared (EO/IR) payload.
    \item \textbf{Kinematics:} Ackermann steering constraints to accurately model minimum turning radii ($r_{min}$) on high-slip surfaces.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Kinematic Platform Selection: Ackermann vs. Differential Drive}
While differential drive architectures simplify trajectory generation, an Ackermann-steered UGV was explicitly selected to satisfy the strict terramechanic and operational constraints of the desert environment. On non-cohesive granular soils (e.g., desert sand), the skid-steering mechanics of differential drives induce severe lateral soil shear, rapidly exceeding Bekker-Wong sinkage thresholds and resulting in immobilization. Conversely, the Ackermann kinematic model allows the UGV to execute continuous-curvature, rolling turns, minimizing soil displacement and preserving tractive effort. Furthermore, Ackermann steering provides superior high-speed longitudinal stability—a critical requirement for executing the sweeping, unpredictable evasion arcs generated by our Lévy Flight planner while minimizing energy expenditure during extended EMCON border patrols.

\subsection{Software Stack}
The framework is built on \textbf{ROS 2}. Localization is handled by LIO-SAM to ensure robust map generation without GNSS dependencies. Global Planning is managed by the Nav2 Smac Hybrid-A* planner, strictly constrained by UGV kinematic limits.

% --- SECTION IV: METHODOLOGY ---
\section{Methodology: Independent Sentinel Logic}

\subsection{Spatially-Bounded Lévy Patrol (SBLP)}
To ensure unpredictable spatial coverage without inter-agent coordination, deterministic waypoints are replaced with a heavy-tailed probability distribution. The step length $l$ is drawn from a Lévy distribution $P(l) \sim l^{-\beta}$, where $1 < \beta \le 3$. To respect UGV kinematics, the algorithm utilizes a rejection sampler that mathematically guarantees the agent remains within a predefined GPS polygon.

\subsection{Perception-Agnostic EMCON via Asynchronous Zenoh}
Relying strictly on RGB networks introduces vulnerabilities against camouflaged adversaries. The architecture abstracts the detection phase into a modular, standardized boolean trigger. Upon anomaly detection, a hardware-coupled state machine activates the transceiver from a Receive-Only (RX) bias. The UGV executes a connectionless UDP micro-burst via the native Eclipse Zenoh API to a centralized Base Station, bypassing DDS discovery storms entirely, before returning to RX-only.

\subsection{Macroscopic Cohesion via RL-Optimized Elastic Geo-Fencing}
Formulated as a Markov Decision Process (MDP), the Base Station's RL agent evaluates the topographic costmap and breach coordinates. The reward function is defined as:
$$R = (W_1 \cdot C_{coverage}) - (W_2 \cdot E_{travel}) - (W_3 \cdot T_{terrain})$$
By broadcasting a single, encrypted Zenoh UDP packet to the remaining fleet, the agents autonomously stretch their SBLP parameters over the optimal terrain, allowing the disconnected fleet to react as a unified macroscopic organism.

% --- SECTION V: EXPERIMENTAL SETUP ---
\section{Experimental Setup}

\subsection{OS-Level Network Emulation}
To empirically validate the EMCON capabilities without relying on physical MANET radios, a Software-in-the-Loop (SITL) approach was utilized. The UGV logic was isolated within Docker containers, partitioned by unique \texttt{ROS\_DOMAIN\_ID}s. The Linux kernel's \texttt{iptables} were dynamically manipulated to emulate the physical severing of the RF datalink.

\subsection{The Physics Environment \& Topographical Generation}
Validation was conducted in \textbf{Gazebo Harmonic}, utilizing the Bullet physics engine to accurately simulate wheel-slip dynamics. To simulate the rolling, non-cohesive terramechanics of a desert border, a 12 $km^2$ Digital Elevation Model (DEM) of the Namibian desert was acquired via satellite telemetry. To induce aggressive but traversable elevation gradients within a computationally viable 900x300 meter simulation boundary, a Spatial Squashing compression technique was applied. 

High-frequency sensor artifacts were eliminated via a Dual-Pass Smoothing Engine: utilizing a spatial median filter to truncate jagged anomalies, followed by a Gaussian blur ($\sigma=8.0$) to synthesize wind-swept topographical continuity. The terrain was geometrically normalized to enforce a maximum elevation amplitude of 25.0 meters, ensuring adherence to the Ackermann UGV's maximum stable climbing pitch. To prevent Sim-to-Real suspension discrepancies, the generated 32-bit floating-point TIFF was explicitly coupled to the physics engine's collision mesh.

\subsection{A Priori GIS Pipeline and Global Costmap}
To replicate pre-mission military intelligence, the continuous 3D topography was analytically projected into a 2D binary occupancy grid. Surface gradients were calculated continuously across the array; pixels exceeding the UGV's $15^\circ$ kinematic climbing threshold were encoded as lethal obstacles. This *a priori* global costmap was provided to the Nav2 Smac planner, enabling the UGV to calculate mathematically optimal macro-routes through traversable valleys prior to dynamic local exploration.

\subsection{Dynamic Asset Optimization for Real-Time Factor (RTF)}
To sustain a simulation RTF $\ge 1.0$ while populating the environment with dense flora, an automated dynamic asset pipeline was engineered. Over 800 vegetation and geological models were procedurally scattered. Using an offline bounding-box pre-analysis, flora falling below the UGV's 0.25m chassis clearance threshold were stripped of collision physics entirely (rendering them purely visual), whereas complex arboreal meshes exceeding the threshold were dynamically substituted with computationally lightweight primitive cylinders.

\section{Conclusion and Future Work}
By decoupling tactical communications from underlying DDS middleware and employing RL for centralized fleet reallocation, the Silent Sentry architecture successfully demonstrates that distributed, partition-based multi-agent systems can maintain highly effective, self-healing border coverage under strict zero-communication constraints. 

Future work will focus on transitioning this architecture from SITL network emulation to physical Jetson Orin Nano platforms equipped with analog MANET radios, alongside field-testing the system's kinematic limits and RL recovery rates in dynamic weather conditions such as severe desert sandstorms.
